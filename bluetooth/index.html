Bluetooth internals

Connection generations, proxy ownership and transport behavior.

View source ↗

A fresh display shows Disconnected while its background scan fills the Bike picker. Scanning without a previously verified bike does not imply a reconnect attempt. Selecting a bike shows Connecting, followed by VESC service discovery and notification subscription. Connected means the Nordic UART Service write and notify characteristics have been found and its notification CCCD write succeeded; telemetry freshness is tracked separately by the application.

The selected candidate remains in memory during bounded retry attempts. Its address is saved to the ble NVS namespace only after notification subscription succeeds. A peer_ok marker records that verification. An older address without that marker can still be tried, but it cannot produce the remembered-peer reconnect state until it has been verified. Forget clears the saved address and marker, invalidates the current stream session and returns to Disconnected.

Saved bike association and Bluetooth SMP bonding serve different purposes. The verified association stores the selected BLE address and vehicle integration; the SMP bond stores connection security keys. A secured upstream device has a separate six-digit pairing prompt. The upstream PIN itself is not saved or reused by the proxy. Persistent bonds allow reconnection without inventing a default code; authentication changes require pairing again. Controller-specific behavior, changed credentials and private-address identity resolution still require hardware acceptance testing.

Asynchronous scan, connect, MTU, GATT discovery and subscription callbacks carry a generation token. A callback from an earlier attempt cannot promote a newer attempt to Connected. Notifications additionally require the current central connection handle, expected UART TX characteristic handle and ready state. Every disconnected, forgotten or paused stream invalidates queued telemetry and writes through its session identifier. OTA pause is displayed explicitly and prevents scanning or connecting until resumed.

The portable lifecycle tests cover fresh repeated scans, first-connect/discovery failures, successful subscription, restoration, disconnect/retry, late callbacks, pause/resume, unsupported authentication and Forget. Hardware testing with actual VESC controllers remains necessary for advertising variants, connection loss, MTU negotiation and security requirements. Controllers using rotating private addresses require identity resolution beyond a saved over-the-air address.

Optional VESC BLE proxy

The BLE proxy setting is off by default. The display uses the standard Nordic UART Service. Its name never mirrors the upstream BLE device or discovered main controller. Active WFB advertises as WFB and hides the editor; wheel-e and generic VESC use the editable saved name, initially WB Disp. BLE and UART provide an ordinary VESC byte stream. CAN exposes a generic adapter with firmware discovery, CAN ping and explicit CAN forwarding; the phone supplies its own controller IDs. The proxy does not consult or rewrite the display client's main-controller route.

The proxy's PIN requirement comes only from the local bike lock PIN. Without a bike PIN it has no PIN requirement. With one, phone pairing requires that six-digit code and authenticated encryption. Changing or removing the bike PIN revokes old phone security sessions and bonds; upstream bonds remain independent. A configured bike PIN requires local unlock after boot before its numeric proxy passkey is available in RAM.

The phone has priority at complete VESC packet boundaries, including before it enables notifications. Read-only display requests continue through transport::poll, with at most one broker-owned poll in flight. In both solo and proxy operation, bounded per-family reply credits expire after one second. Broker-owned replies update display telemetry and are not forwarded to the phone. Package command 36 and BMS command 96 require this ownership. A phone command-47 setup reply remains on its phone stream and may also update the display if its controller ID at payload offset 56 matches the discovered controller or known solo ID; native CAN also requires the matching source. Such packets are marked Packet.passive_proxy and do not count as owned-request RTT measurements. Replies without that proof cannot replace dashboard data.

After three complete phone packets, a waiting display poll can reserve its exact reply key for up to 200 ms; ingress queue pressure ends the reservation early. Queued requests with the same key are deduplicated. An unsent poll whose reservation expires is skipped rather than disconnecting the phone. An unfinished phone packet or phone firmware update temporarily suppresses injected display polls. Initial main-controller discovery still waits until the phone disconnects if discovery was incomplete when it connected. Configuration writes and display firmware maintenance remain blocked throughout the phone connection. BMS command 96 gets an initial read-only probe when the display becomes Ready, then polls at 1 Hz while Stats is open. During a phone connection, it is polled only if a command-96 reply has already established support in the current vehicle session.

The relay uses three 16 KiB byte rings in PSRAM for phone ingress, upstream receive and phone output. Before forwarding any bytes of a phone frame, it waits for the complete frame and validates length, terminator and CRC. The phone-transmit limit is 16 KiB including framing, not unlimited packet size; ordinary VESC uses 512-byte payloads. An invalid or oversized phone frame ends the phone session before bytes from that frame reach the bike. Native CAN additionally applies its bounded packet codec and reframes replies for the phone.

Validated frames are coalesced into batches of up to 512 bytes while retaining frame boundaries and the guards for matching reply keys and firmware-transfer conflicts. Different phone/upstream MTUs are handled without adding a fixed wait per ATT fragment. Nonblocking phone-notification service retains unsent output, so notification pressure cannot stall phone-to-bike transmission. Display poll admission does not depend on phone authentication or notification subscription: an idle or pairing phone cannot stop local read-only polls, and an unauthenticated phone receives no data.

A normal phone disconnect finishes already admitted valid frames and discards a never-forwarded partial tail. After backend transmission drains and upstream receive becomes quiet, the display resumes with the existing bike BLE session and main-controller route intact. It does not reset the bike connection merely because the phone left. This does not assume an idle timeout in the controller parser: the official VESC packet.c parser has none. Source loss, corruption and unrecoverable transfer failures remain exceptional recovery paths. Epoch checks keep work from previous sessions out of replacement connections.

On the BLE backend, unaccepted ENOMEM and EBUSY writes retry the same bytes for up to two seconds, yielding between attempts. NimBLE already accepts controller-credit EAGAIN internally, so accepted writes are never resent. Display OTA reserves proxy admission before starting; a connected phone blocks OTA, while a later phone cannot acquire the reserved link.

The proxy provides the phone the VESC access normally available over its BLE dongle, including configuration and firmware commands. Enable it only when that access is wanted. Its pairing requirement follows the local bike PIN policy; this is separate from upstream BLE credentials. Hardware validation with a real VESC and phone app is required for discovery, both MTUs, sustained transfers, disconnects and app firmware-update behavior. Portable tests verify the ownership, default-off, maintenance and stale-stream policy, not radio interoperability.

Discovery prioritizes names containing WFB or wheel-e (case insensitive), while other NUS devices, VESC names and the saved peer remain selectable. Known scan candidates can receive name updates from separate scan-response packets. A matching device can displace a generic candidate when the six-entry cache is full. The UI sorts a snapshot; selection resolves the displayed BLE address under the central mutex rather than using a potentially reordered scan index.

The verified association stores the BLE address, selected integration policy and last connected name in one versioned NVS blob. Auto is the default and remains selected after discovery. It probes WFB and wheel-e packages on discovered motor controllers, then selects the first verified match. The probes run again on reconnect, including for renamed devices. Names only rank discovery candidates. When neither supported package is found, Auto can use read-only generic VESC telemetry. Mismatched custom replies are rejected and cannot enable writes.

New proxy advertising is held until the main route is verified plus a 300 ms settling period, or until the 10-second admission timeout if discovery cannot finish. An existing phone connection is left intact. This gives initial discovery a chance to finish before phone auto-connect can defer it. Only the active WFB integration forces WFB in both GAP and its scan response and hides the BLE-name editor. Other integrations use the saved editable name. Switching away from WFB restores that preference, without changing the NVS value. The bridge is paused while the local screen lock is active, for either PIN or pattern entry. Explicit locking disconnects the phone, and successful local unlock resumes proxy admission subject to route and security readiness. This local access control does not authenticate a client's separate direct connection to the bike.

Encrypted BLE and credential changes

VESC Express's standard VESC service uses SMP with a fixed six-digit passkey when encrypted mode is selected. Its NUS characteristics require authenticated encryption. The display acts as a keyboard-capable central and prompts for the upstream PIN; a successful bond stores security keys rather than the entered PIN. This is separate from the scripting BLE interface, whose capabilities are different.

The implementation reference is VESC Express comm_ble.c at 511a026. Pairing retries must reject stale connection handles and remove rejected peer bonds rather than repeatedly replaying an old credential. An unencrypted upstream connection is not forced into SMP just because an earlier configuration used a PIN.

The proxy always follows the local bike PIN policy. Removing upstream BLE encryption therefore does not remove a configured proxy PIN, and changing the bike PIN does not overwrite the upstream bond. Real-device verification remains necessary for failed pairing, changed credentials, restored bonds, repeat pairing and phone-side cached-bond behavior.

The header keeps bike connection/fault information as its primary text. An authenticated phone connection appears as a green smartphone icon immediately left of Settings. The icon is absent while only advertising or waiting for phone authentication. While an authorized phone is connected, the header reports a healthy ready vehicle link as Connected. Telemetry retains its own freshness checks; temporarily suppressed polls can still leave individual readings unavailable. Disconnection/unavailability is red, pending or stale state yellow, and healthy current connections green.
